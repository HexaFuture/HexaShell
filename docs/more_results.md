# More results

**English | [简体中文](more_results_zh.md)**

RoboColiseum Season I, RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO-Pro 2.0 and LIBERO-PRO: how to run them, their protocols and our results. The
other benchmarks and the real robots are in the [README](../README.md#results). Commands run from the root of the
repository; [scripts/setup/README.md](../scripts/setup/README.md) describes the environments.

Success rates (%) with `gpt-6-astra` at reasoning effort `high`, October–November 2026. RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO-Pro 2.0 and
LIBERO-PRO are our own evaluations; RoboColiseum's platform runs and scores its episodes. Without skills, nothing is written
for any task; with skills, the task's page of guidance is added to the prompt. "Other results": the best entry of the
benchmark's leaderboard or results reported in papers, each on its own episodes and protocol.

## RoboColiseum Season I

Genie Sim 3.5, AgiBot G2 with omnipicker grippers; four boards, `instruction`, `spatial`, `manip` and `robust`; 12 skills
([skills/robocoliseum1/](../skills/robocoliseum1/)). As in [Season II](../README.md#robocoliseum-eval), the benchmark is the
client and HexaShell answers its requests, locally or on the competition's platform. Local episodes are split into learning,
validation and test parts by layout, and a run opens a validation or test layout only when the part is named (`--part`).
Skills are on with `HEXASHELL_SKILLS=1`.

```bash
source config/env.sh
# local, with Genie Sim 3.5: one layout, all its instructions
HEXASHELL_SKILLS=1 $HEXASHELL_PYTHON -m hexashell.benches.robocoliseum1.run --config g2op_if_pick_block_color --layout 0
# online, without a simulator: submit a job on a board, then K agents for it
$HEXASHELL_PYTHON -m hexashell.benches.robocoliseum1.challenge login
$HEXASHELL_PYTHON -m hexashell.benches.robocoliseum1.challenge submit --board spatial --name <name>    # --yes sends it
bash hexashell/benches/robocoliseum1/online.sh spatial 4 results/online_spatial
```

## RoboTwin 2.0

The same runners and scoring as for [RoboCasa365](../README.md#robocasa-eval), with `robotwin` as the first word of each list
line:

```bash
source config/env.sh
$HEXASHELL_PYTHON -m hexashell.benches.run --bench robotwin --task adjust_bottle --seed 100000 --set skills=yes \
    --out results/demo
# the test list: 750 episodes
mkdir -p logs && $HEXASHELL_PYTHON -c 'from hexashell.benches.robotwin.protocol import test_episodes
for task, seed in test_episodes(): print("robotwin", task, seed)' > logs/twin_test.todo
SETS="skills=yes" python3 scripts/bench_pool.py twin_test 0,0,1,1 4
python3 scripts/bench_score.py results/twin_test
```

The test follows RoboTwin's own evaluation of the clean2random setting: the `demo_randomized` scenes, each task's
step limit (400–1700 steps; one step is one `take_action`, the joint targets of both arms of the aloha-agilex robot),
an instruction from the task's unseen description templates, and per task the first seeds from 100000 that
RoboTwin's expert solves, found as RoboTwin's evaluation finds them (`scripts/setup/scan_robotwin_seeds.py`; the
result is `hexashell/benches/robotwin/seeds.json`). We take the first 15 per task: 750 episodes, with at most 40
replies and two hours each. Seeds below 100000 are for development, 10 for learning and 10 for validation per task,
with instructions from the seen templates; the skills use only those wordings. The tasks are the 50 of RPent's
evaluation list (`eval_seeds.json`, see NOTICE). Scenes are built with three fixes to RoboTwin's runtime (see
`hexashell/benches/robotwin/protocol.py`). Perception uses the colour pictures and the camera models only, as on
RoboDojo (`--set depth=true` reads the depth pictures as well). Both test runs used the settings of `config/benches.yml`
with a picture window of 20/14.

- **With skills 726/750 (96.8%), without them 637/750 (84.9%)** on the same episodes: 101 gained, 12 lost. With skills 36
  tasks pass 15/15; in 29 tasks both runs do.
- The 15 tasks whose skills were revised after the validation round scored 205/225 with skills and 142/225 without, the
  other 35 tasks 521/525 and 495/525. Without skills the two tasks that pick up a bottle with each arm
  (pick_diverse_bottles, pick_dual_bottles) fail all 15 episodes and place_dual_shoes fails 14; with skills each of the
  three passes 14.
- Of the 24 failures with skills the model ended 14 itself, 7 ran out of replies and 3 out of steps; of the 113 without,
  52, 6 and 55. An episode took 8.4 replies on average with skills and 12.0 without.
- We did not run clean2clean (`demo_clean`, seen instructions).

## LIBERO-Pro 2.0

[LIBERO-Pro 2.0](https://github.com/LIBERO-Pro-Team/LIBERO-Pro2.0) ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24587)) perturbs
LIBERO's tasks one way at a time: 22 static perturbations, in place from the start, and 20 dynamic ones, events during
the episode, in six categories. Its test split holds 339 instances of eight tasks, two per suite. Every instance starts
from initial state 0, and the seed sets the draws of the perturbation. Of the 30 seeds of the benchmark's example
evaluation (142–171) we use 142–146 for learning, 147–151 for validation and 152–171 for the test. The reported test is
the first ten, 152–161: 3,390 episodes.

The adapter follows the official evaluation: the same reset, the official actions of relative hand pose and gripper through
the benchmark's own step function, its step limits (220, 280, 300 and 520 steps at 20 Hz for Spatial, Object, Goal and
LIBERO-10) and its success check after every step; besides, at most 40 replies and two hours. The agent sees the front and
the wrist camera, RGB pictures of 512 pixels without depth, and the observation's readings. Its camera models are the
nominal ones, which camera perturbations make wrong. It is not told which perturbation is active: the task card names the
suite, and the current instruction comes with every reply.

```bash
source config/env.sh
$HEXASHELL_PYTHON -m hexashell.benches.run --bench liberopro2 --task libero_goal_t3_S15 --seed 142 --set skills=yes \
    --out results/demo
mkdir -p logs && python3 scripts/liberopro2_lists.py test --seeds 152-161 > logs/libero2_test.todo   # 3390 episodes
SETS="skills=yes" python3 scripts/bench_pool.py libero2_test 0,0,1,1 4
python3 scripts/bench_score.py results/libero2_test      # the leaderboard's scores: Base, Static, Dynamic, Overall
```

A task is named `<suite>_t<i>_<case>`; the test tasks are spatial 0 and 8, object 1 and 8, goal 3 and 6, libero_10 5 and
8. `skills/liberopro2/` has one skill per task, for all its perturbations. A cell is a suite under one perturbation; Base
is the mean over the four unperturbed cells, Static over 87 cells and Dynamic over 80, the categories over their cells,
and Overall over all 167 perturbed cells, as on the leaderboard (success rate, %; the leaderboard's entries as of
17 November 2026, each on its own episodes):

- **Counts.** 2,572 of 3,390 episodes without skills and 3,090 with them; the skills gain 573 episodes and lose 55. On all
  20 test seeds, which only the version with skills ran, the overall rate is 90.9 (6,191 of 6,780 episodes).
- One task accounts for more than half of the difference: in `libero_goal_t3` (open the top drawer and put the bowl inside)
  the agent without skills fails all 420 episodes, and with them it succeeds in 322.
- With skills an episode took 6.1 replies and 95 s on average, without them 8.5 replies and 119 s. Of the 300 failures with
  skills, 226 ran out of steps and the agent ended 74.
- After the test, the skills of the cells that failed most were revised. The revisions were checked only on new seeds
  (172–191) and do not enter the results above; `skills/liberopro2/` holds the revised skills.

## LIBERO-PRO

The same runners and scoring as for [RoboCasa365](../README.md#robocasa-eval), with `libero` as the first word of each list
line:

```bash
source config/env.sh
$HEXASHELL_PYTHON -m hexashell.benches.run --bench libero --task libero_goal_swap_t3 --seed 20 --set skills=yes \
    --out results/demo
# the test list: 1560 episodes
mkdir -p logs && $HEXASHELL_PYTHON -c 'from hexashell.benches.libero.protocol import TEST_SEEDS, test_tasks
for task in test_tasks():
    for seed in TEST_SEEDS: print("libero", task, seed)' > logs/libero_test.todo
SETS="skills=yes" python3 scripts/bench_pool.py libero_test 0,0,1,1 4
python3 scripts/bench_score.py results/libero_test
```

LIBERO-PRO perturbs each of LIBERO's four suites (spatial, object, goal, 10) in one way at a time. We test four of
its perturbations: the objects start in exchanged places (`_swap`), the objects look different (`_object`), the
instruction is reworded (`_lan`), and another task in the same scene (`_task`): 16 suites of ten tasks
(`<suite>_t<i>`, e.g. `libero_goal_swap_t3`), of which four have no initial states in LIBERO-PRO's release, so 156
tasks. Seed `s` starts from the task's initial state `s`; seeds 0–9 are for learning, 10–19 for validation, 20–29 the
test: 1560 episodes. The scene settles for 10 control steps, as in LIBERO-PRO's own evaluation; then its step limit
for the suite applies (20 Hz control steps: 220 for spatial, 280 object, 300 goal, 520 for libero_10), with at most
40 replies and two hours. Success is LIBERO's goal check. The 71 tasks with a skill are those that failed in the
learning round (no skills) or in the validation round. The skills were learned and checked on development seeds; no record
of a test seed went into them. The test used the settings of benches.yml with skills on and a picture window of 20/14:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
